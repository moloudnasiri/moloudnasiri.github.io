I study how people move through virtual worlds, and how large language models reason about math.

I earned my Ph.D. in Human-Centered Computing at Clemson University in 2023. My work covers VR locomotion and navigation, gait, perception, and tangible interfaces. More recently it has included research on LLMs. I have taught computer science at Converse University and Florida Polytechnic University.

Research

Mathematical reasoning and transfer in large language models

How LLM reasoning and transfer in math are organized by problem-solving approach rather than by topic. A manuscript is under review at ICLR 2027, and the preprint is on arXiv.

2026, collaborative research

VR locomotion, navigation, gait, and perception

I built VR environments in Unity and ran user studies with HTC Vive and Oculus Quest headsets. I compared joystick and teleportation locomotion over four weeks, and studied how prior VR experience relates to walking in real and virtual environments. I also studied visual cues for perceiving pivot-door rotation axes, sensory channels in near-field size perception, and visual feedback in a virtual basketball-shooting task.

2019 to 2023, Virtual Environments Lab, Clemson University

Tangible and interactive systems

I modeled and fabricated physical prototypes, including 3D printing on fabric and laser cutting. I designed Enodia, a highly reconfigurable instrument for collaborative engagement with screen-mediated content.

2017 to 2019, Tangible Visualization Lab, Clemson University

Computer engineering and nanoelectronics

I designed a ternary serial adder and a flash analog-to-digital converter from carbon nanotube field-effect transistors, using VHDL, Verilog, and HSPICE simulation.

2013 to 2015, Nanotechnology and Quantum Computing Lab, Beheshti University
